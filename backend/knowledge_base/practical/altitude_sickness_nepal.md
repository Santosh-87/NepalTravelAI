## Altitude Sickness Nepal

High Altitude Sickness, also known as "Acute Mountain Sickness" or "AMS," is a condition that happens by a deficiency of oxygen at a high altitude. Mountain sickness is another name for it. It is not possible to determine in advance who might experience symptoms of altitude sickness. Because it is possible for anyone to be impacted, we are unable to determine who will be affected based on characteristics such as gender, age, or even level of physical fitness because it can happen to anyone. Symptoms almost never become apparent at altitudes lower than 2500 meters (8202 ft.)

In fact, at an altitude of 2000 meters, small problems like shortness of breath could happen. Mountain sickness, on the other hand, occurs when we abruptly ascend to a high height exceeding 2500 m. Thousands of people have died as a result of a lack of awareness. It is not a fatal illness that can strike anyone. However, you should take precautions when ascending above 2500 meters. It is fairly simple to prevent, as we will discuss later.

If you go to a different altitude too rapidly, you may suffer from altitude sickness. Because you can't inhale much oxygen, breathing becomes difficult. If not treated for altitude sickness on time, commonly known as Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS), it can bring you into a medical emergency. You can suffer from altitude sickness no matter how old you are, what gender you are, or how fit are you. Also, if you haven't had it before, that doesn't mean you will not get it on another adventure.

## Altitude Sickness Symptoms

Altitude sickness symptoms usually start 6 to 24 hours after getting to a height of more than 2,500 meters above sea level. The symptoms of altitude sickness are comparable to those of a terrible hangover and include the following:
- Painful headaches
- Being ill and feeling unwell
- Loss of appetite
- Breathing problems


The body's signs and symptoms are frequently more severe at night.

Mountain climbers aren't the only ones who get altitude sickness. Tourists traveling to lower popular trekking routes such as Poon Hill Trek (3200 M) or Mohare Danda Trek (3300 M) in Nepal also can get altitude sickness. It is all determined according to the type of destination and the pace of your walk in the mountains.

However, the altitude in the lower Terai is not very high it is impossible to suffer from altitude sickness in places like Kathmandu, Pokhara, Chitwan, Lumbini, Palpa, and so on.

## How to Prevent Altitude Sickness?

Traveling carefully to altitudes above 2,500 meters is the best approach to avoiding altitude sickness. Your body usually needs a few days to get used to and acclimatize to the altitude.

You should also consider to prevent from Altitude Sickness:
- If you can, try not to fly straight to areas of a high altitude.
- Allow two to three days to acclimate to high elevations before venturing above 2,500 meters.
- Keep your daily ascents to 300-500 meters.
- Take a day off for every 600 to 900 meters you climb or every three to four days.
- Make sure you're getting enough water.
- Give up smoking and alcohol.
- Refrain from engaging in vigorous physical activity for the first twenty-four hours.
- consume a high-calorie, low-carbohydrate diet

## 1. High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE)

An accumulation of fluid in the lungs, known as HAPE, can be extremely hazardous and even fatal. The most prevalent medical evidence of altitude sickness is heart failure.
- A blue sign on the skin or lips
- Difficulty breathing even when resting
- Chest constrictions
- A respiratory infection is accompanied by the generation of frothy pink or white fluids (sputum).
- Fatigue and weakness


HAPE symptoms might occur as soon as arriving at a high elevation. If not treated right away, it can be incurable.
- Immediately go down to a lower elevation.
- Utilize nifedipine.
- Provide bottled oxygen if it is accessible.


Nifedipine is a medication that helps to relieve chest pain and improve breathing. It's also frequently included in medical supplies on expeditions.

How long does it take to recover from altitude sickness?

Most people recover from mild altitude sickness within 1-3 days, with symptoms usually improving significantly within 24 hours if they rest and descend to a lower altitude however, severe cases may take longer to resolve and could require medical attention.

Key points about altitude sickness recovery:

Mild symptoms: Headache, nausea, fatigue, dizziness - typically improve within a day or two with rest and staying at a lower altitude.

When to descend: If symptoms worsen or do not improve within a day, immediately descend to a lower altitude.

Severe cases: High-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE) or high-altitude cerebral edema (HACE) can be life-threatening and require immediate medical attention.
- Severity of symptoms: More severe symptoms take longer to resolve.
- Rate of ascent: Rapid ascent to high altitudes increases the risk of altitude sickness.
- Individual susceptibility: Some people are more prone to altitude sickness than others.


Altitude sickness often feels worse at night, and struggling to sleep, feeling like every breath was a little too shallow. When youre lying down, blood flow to the brain becomes less efficient, making the already low oxygen levels at high altitudes even more noticeable. This can intensify symptoms like headaches, dizziness, and shortness of breath, making rest feel impossible. On top of that, the body's natural breathing patterns slow down during sleep, further reducing oxygen intake. Frequent awakenings, vivid dreams, or even a sensation of gasping for air can make the night feel endless, adding to the overall discomfort. This is why many climbers and trekkers find nights at high altitudes far more challenging than the days.

Breathing at high altitude requires a deliberate approach to help your body adjust to lower oxygen levels. The key is to take slow, deep breaths through your nose, allowing for maximum oxygen intake while keeping breathing steady. Pursed-lip breathingwhere you exhale slowly through pursed lips, as if blowing out a candlehelps control airflow and prevents rapid, shallow breathing that can make altitude sickness symptoms worse.

Another important technique is diaphragmatic breathing, which means focusing on filling your belly rather than just your chest, engaging the diaphragm for better lung efficiency. Its a simple adjustment that makes a big difference. Practicing these breathing techniques before heading to high altitudes can be helpful, as it trains your body to breathe more efficiently when oxygen is scarce. In the mountains, every breath matters, and learning to breathe properly can make acclimatization smoother and trekking much more comfortable.

Does drinking water help with altitude sickness?

Yes, staying hydrated can help prevent altitude sickness. At high altitudes, your body loses water more quickly, and dehydration can worsen symptoms.

How to Prevent Altitude Sickness by Staying Hydrated:
- Start early: Drink plenty of water before traveling to high altitudes to stay ahead of dehydration.
- Increase intake: Aim to drink twice as much water as you normally would at lower elevations.
- Stay consistent: Sip water regularly throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts all at once.


Oxygen levels in the atmosphere start to noticeably decline at around 8,000 feet (2,500 meters) above sea level, which is generally the point where the effects of lower oxygen levels, such as altitude sickness, become more apparent. However, sensitivity variessome individuals may experience symptoms even at lower elevations, especially if they ascend rapidly or have a predisposition to altitude sickness. As elevation increases, the available oxygen continues to drop, making acclimatization crucial for avoiding discomfort and more serious altitude-related conditions.
