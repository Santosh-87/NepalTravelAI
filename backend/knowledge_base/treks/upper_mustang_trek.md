# Upper Mustang Trek

**Source:** https://www.nepalhikingteam.com/package/upper-mustang-trek
**Last updated:** February 27, 2026

---

## Overview

The Upper Mustang Trek is a 17-day journey into Nepal's "Last Forbidden Kingdom" — one of the most remote and culturally preserved regions in the world. Upper Mustang remained closed to outsiders until 1992 and still requires a special restricted area permit, keeping visitor numbers low and the region pristine.

- **Destination:** Nepal (Mustang District, Far North)
- **Duration:** 17 Days
- **Difficulty:** Easy to Moderate
- **Max. Elevation:** 4,280m (Chogo La Pass)
- **Best Time:** March–May & September–November
- **Trekking Distance:** 135–150 km (starts and ends in Jomsom)
- **Minimum Group Size:** 2 persons (restricted area requirement)
- **Access:** Kathmandu → Pokhara (bus) → Jomsom (20-min flight)

Upper Mustang is known as the "Last Forbidden Kingdom" — a Tibetan-influenced region with centuries-old monasteries, ancient cave dwellings, walled medieval cities, and dramatic red-cliff desert landscapes that feel completely unlike anywhere else in Nepal.

---

## Key Highlights

- Explore Lo-Manthang (3,810m) — the ancient walled capital of the Kingdom of Lo, with palaces and monasteries dating back to the 14th century
- Cross Chogo La Pass (4,280m) — the highest point of the trek, with sweeping views of Mustang's desert landscape
- Visit Namgyal Gompa — a 14th–15th century Buddhist monastery on a hill above Lo-Manthang
- Explore Lo-Gekar (Ghar Gompa) — an 8th-century monastery built by Guru Padmasambhava, believed to be older than Tibet's Samye Monastery
- Receive blessings at Muktinath Temple — one of Nepal's holiest Hindu and Buddhist pilgrimage sites with 108 holy water spouts and an eternal flame
- Walk alongside Mustang's longest Mani wall — thousands of prayer-carved stones added over centuries
- Discover ancient cliff caves built into dramatic red and ochre cliffs throughout the valley
- Spot Saligram stones — sacred fossils prized by both Hindus and Buddhists, found along the Kali Gandaki
- Visit Kagbeni — historic 14th-century checkpoint village between Mustang and the rest of Nepal
- Experience traditional Tibetan-influenced villages: Tangbe, Ghemi, Tsarang, Chhusang, Charang
- Thrilling 20-minute scenic mountain flight Pokhara to Jomsom over Annapurna and Dhaulagiri

---

## Best Time to Trek

- **Spring (March–May):** Clear mountain views, stable weather, some festivals
- **Autumn (September–November):** Dry trails, best visibility, most popular season
- **Summer (June–August):** Less crowded, longer daylight, colorful wildflowers — Upper Mustang lies in a rain shadow so monsoon has minimal impact here compared to other Nepal treks

Winter (December–February) is very cold but possible for experienced trekkers.

---

## Permits Required

Upper Mustang is a **restricted area** — special permits required, minimum 2 trekkers mandatory:

- **Upper Mustang Restricted Area Permit (RAP)** — USD 500 per person for the first 10 days, USD 50 per day after that
- **Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)**
- **TIMS Card**

All permits must be arranged before entering the restricted zone at Kagbeni checkpoint.

## Detailed Itinerary

- **Day 01:** Arrival in Kathmandu — airport transfer, rest
- **Day 02:** Kathmandu Valley sightseeing — Swayambhunath, Boudhanath, Pashupatinath, Kathmandu Durbar Square (all UNESCO World Heritage Sites)
- **Day 03:** Drive Kathmandu to Pokhara — 200 km, 6–7 hours along Trisuli River via Prithvi Highway; explore Lakeside and Fewa Lake
- **Day 04:** Fly Pokhara to Jomsom (20 min), trek to Kagbeni — 10–11 km, 3 hours; Kagbeni was a 14th-century trading checkpoint for Tibet-Nepal trade
- **Day 05:** Trek Kagbeni to Chele (3,050m) — 13 km, 5–6 hours; pass Tangbe village (white-washed Tibetan-style houses, apple orchards), Chhusang (natural rock tunnel formed by fallen cliff), steep climb to Chele
- **Day 06:** Trek Chele to Geling (3,570m) — 19 km, 5–6 hours; landscape transitions to high desert; pass Samar village, cross ridge at 3,800m, descend to Shyangmochen, climb final pass at 3,770m into Geling
- **Day 07:** Trek Geling to Charang (3,560m) — 16 km, 7–8 hours; ascend to Nyi La Pass (4,010m) with dramatic blue, red, and grey cliff views; walk alongside Mustang's longest Mani wall; pass old statues and thangka paintings; descend to Charang
- **Day 08:** Trek Charang to Lo-Manthang (3,810m) — 12 km, 4–5 hours; visit Lo-Gekar Monastery (8th-century, one of the oldest in the region); first view of Lo-Manthang plateau
- **Day 09:** Exploration day in Lo-Manthang — hike to Namgyal Gompa (3,930m), 2 hours; visit Tingkhar village (northwesternmost settlement, ~40 families); explore ancient palaces and monasteries within the walled city
- **Day 10:** Trek Lo-Manthang to Dhakmar via Chogo La Pass (4,280m) — 17 km, 6–7 hours; highest point of trek; visit Ghar Gompa (Lo-Gekar, 8th-century monastery built by Guru Padmasambhava); dramatic cliff descent to Dhakmar
- **Day 11:** Trek Dhakmar to Shyangmochen (3,800m) — 12–14 km, 5–6 hours; descend via lower Ghami (3,520m), pass through Chhung-Kah and Tama Gaon villages
- **Day 12:** Trek Shyangmochen to Chhusang (2,980m) — 11–12 km, 6 hours; optional detour to Ranchung Cave (sacred Buddhist cave used by ancient lamas for meditation); dramatic red cliffs at Chhusang
- **Day 13:** Trek Chhusang to Muktinath (3,760m) — 15–18 km, 7 hours; ascend via Tetang (3,040m) and Gyu La Pass (4,077m); spot Saligram stones along the Kali Gandaki; views of Annapurna, Dhaulagiri, and Tukuche Himal
- **Day 14:** Morning visit to Muktinath Temple; trek to Jomsom (2,720m) — 20–25 km, 6 hours; visit Jharkot village (monastery, old fortress, Tibetan medical center); descend along Kali Gandaki Valley past Kagbeni to Jomsom
- **Day 15:** Fly Jomsom to Pokhara (20–25 min); explore Lakeside and Phewa Lake
- **Day 16:** Drive Pokhara to Kathmandu — 200 km, 6–7 hours; receive trek completion certificate on arrival
- **Day 17:** Departure from Tribhuvan International Airport

## Difficulty & Fitness

Rated easy to moderate. No technical climbing required. The main challenges are altitude, remoteness, and daily walking distances.

Most demanding sections:
- **Geling to Charang (Day 7):** Longest walking day — 16 km, 7–8 hours with Nyi La Pass (4,010m)
- **Lo-Manthang to Dhakmar via Chogo La (Day 10):** Highest point at 4,280m, steep descent
- **Chhusang to Muktinath (Day 13):** Long climb via Gyu La Pass (4,077m)

Key notes:
- Minimum 2 trekkers — restricted area regulation
- Gradual ascent profile allows natural acclimatization
- Upper Mustang lies in a Himalayan rain shadow — less affected by monsoon than other Nepal treks

## Lo-Manthang — The Walled Capital

Lo-Manthang (3,810m) is the ancient capital of the Kingdom of Lo, founded in the 14th century. The walled city contains:
- **Royal Palace** — home of the King of Mustang (the kingdom lost autonomous status in 2008)
- **Namgyal Gompa** — 14th–15th century monastery restored by monk Ngorchen Kunga Zangpo
- **Thubchen Gompa** — large assembly hall with ancient murals
- **Chodey Gompa** — oldest monastery within the walled city
- **Tingkhar village** — northwesternmost settlement, ~40 families

---

## Muktinath Temple

- One of the holiest pilgrimage sites in Nepal for Hindus and Buddhists
- Hindu deity: Lord Vishnu (16th-century idol)
- Buddhist recognition: Chenrizeg (Avalokiteshvara)
- Features 108 holy water spouts — pilgrims bathe for spiritual purification
- Eternal flame burns at the entrance (Jwala Mai shrine)
- Adjacent Mebar Lha Gompa (small Buddhist monastery)
- Jharkot village nearby has a Tibetan medical center offering Ayurvedic treatments

## Trail & Landscape

Upper Mustang lies in the rain shadow of the Himalayas, creating a unique high-altitude desert landscape unlike anywhere else in Nepal:

| Section | Terrain & Features |
|---|---|
| Jomsom to Kagbeni | Kali Gandaki River valley, wind-swept desert |
| Kagbeni to Chele | Tibetan-style villages, natural rock tunnel, apple orchards |
| Chele to Lo-Manthang | High desert plateaus, red and ochre cliffs, Mani walls, ancient monasteries |
| Lo-Manthang area | Walled medieval city, cave dwellings, royal palaces |
| Return via Chogo La | Highest pass, dramatic cliff scenery, Ghar Gompa |
| Chhusang to Muktinath | Sacred Saligram stones, views of Dhaulagiri and Annapurna |

## Essential Packing List

- Daypack 25–30L with rain cover
- Sleeping bag rated to –15°C or lower
- Down jacket (essential above 3,500m and for cold desert nights)
- 2L water bottles or hydration reservoir
- Headlamp with spare batteries
- Trekking boots (waterproof, ankle support)
- Layered clothing: base layer, mid layer, waterproof outer shell
- Warm hat, buff, gloves
- Sunscreen SPF 50+ and lip balm (high UV in desert altitude)
- Sunglasses (UV400 — intense desert sun)
- Trekking poles (recommended)
- Dust mask or buff (strong winds and dust common in Kali Gandaki Valley)
- Personal first aid and altitude sickness medication
- Personal travel insurance including helicopter evacuation (essential)

---

## Flight Note — Jomsom to Pokhara

The Jomsom–Pokhara flight takes 20–25 minutes. Flights are frequently delayed or cancelled due to strong afternoon winds in the Kali Gandaki Valley — one of the world's windiest valleys. Always book the earliest morning flight available. If cancelled, the overland route via Marpha, Ghasa, Tatopani, and Beni to Pokhara takes approximately 5–6 hours by jeep.
